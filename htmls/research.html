How to read this page

Study type first, finding second.

Every card below carries a tier. The coloured spine on the card is that tier, not the infusion — so a preclinical animal study never looks like a meta-analysis, whichever treatment it sits under.

  1. TIER 01

    Systematic review / meta-analysis

    Pools every qualifying study on a question. The strongest tier here.

    0 of 27 references
  2. TIER 02

    Clinical study in people

    Measured an outcome in human participants.

    0 of 27 references
  3. TIER 03

    Peer-reviewed review

    Summarises a field. Depends on the studies underneath it.

    0 of 27 references
  4. TIER 04

    Observational study

    Describes an association in a population. Not cause and effect.

    0 of 27 references
  5. TIER 05

    Preclinical — animal or cell

    Not conducted in people. Findings may not carry over.

    0 of 27 references

Featured paper

What is homocysteine — and why does it matter?

A 2016 researcher report from the Alzheimer’s Drug Discovery Foundation reviewing the published literature on B6, B12, folate, homocysteine biology and cognitive aging. It is honest about what the evidence shows and what it does not — the kind of document Alpha Vital recommends reading before any wellness conversation about B vitamins.

The paper explains that high homocysteine levels may be associated with:

  • Cognitive decline
  • Brain aging
  • Vascular stress
  • Increased neurological risk

B6, B12 and folate are the B‑vitamins the body uses to metabolise and lower homocysteine. The report goes further than that, though — it walks through meta-analyses, randomised trials, and the limits of B‑vitamin supplementation in healthy individuals versus people with deficiency. It is a careful read, not a marketing summary.

What this library is not

A citation is not a promise.

Most of this is not IV research

The majority of published work on these compounds studies oral or dietary intake, and much of it studies a specific disease population. Neither is the same as an infusion given to a well person, and we will not pretend otherwise.

Association is not cause

Observational and cross-sectional entries describe a pattern in a population. They cannot tell you that one thing produced the other, and they are labelled tier 04 here for exactly that reason.

Nothing here is a claim about you

These references describe why a compound is of clinical interest. Whether an infusion is appropriate for you is decided by Dr. Aronov after reviewing your history, medications and current clinical picture — not by a study.
