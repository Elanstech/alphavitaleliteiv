How does a molecule reach its intended destination?

A question that has shaped a career in science and medicine I first explored this question in a biochemistry laboratory at SUNY Stony Brook, studying hyaluronic acid polymers engineered to deliver drugs, vitamins and enzymes. Decades later, that same scientific curiosity guides every infusion decision in my practice: What belongs in the infusion? In what amount? At what rate? And, before anything enters the bloodstream, is an IV medically appropriate for the individual in front of me? Precision is not simply knowing what can be infused. It is knowing what should be infused — and for whom.
Chapter I The career path

From the laboratory bench, through the operating room, to Alpha Vital Elite Precision Medical IV.

Where did Dr. Aronov’s path to physician-directed IV care begin? Not with a wellness trend. At a laboratory bench. Before she ever entered an operating room, she was already investigating a question that would shape the way she thought about medicine: once a substance enters the body, where does it go — and what happens next?

1995 Undergraduate research 01 / 05

1995 — 1999

A degree — and the problem of delivery

SUNY at Stony Brook · B.S. in Biochemistry

What was the challenge?
It was not simply choosing a drug, vitamin or enzyme. The challenge was finding a way to carry it through the body toward the tissue where it was intended to act.

What did she study?
Dr. Aronov’s undergraduate research explored how hyaluronic acid–based polymer systems could serve as delivery vehicles for drugs, vitamins and enzymes.

What stayed with her?
The substance itself is only part of the story. Its route, delivery, behavior inside the body, and the individual patient’s response all help shape what happens next.

Introducing a compound into the body is only the first step. Understanding what happens next is where the real science — and the responsibility — begin. That principle would follow Dr. Aronov from the research bench into the operating room, and ultimately into the practice she founded.

1997 — 1999

What tells an injured nerve to begin repairing itself?

Regeneron Pharmaceuticals · Tarrytown, NY

The question was microscopic. Its implications were anything but.

During the final two years of her biochemistry degree at SUNY Stony Brook, Dr. Aronov entered pharmaceutical research at Regeneron, studying the cellular science behind nerve repair and recovery after injury.

There, she saw how discoveries made inside a laboratory could one day change what happens at a patient’s bedside. That possibility became the bridge that carried her from research into medicine.

1999 — 2004

Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine

NYCOM at New York Institute of Technology

At NYCOM, Dr. Aronov learned to look beyond the location of pain and ask a more revealing question: what else in the body may be influencing it?

Her osteopathic medical training emphasized anatomy, the musculoskeletal system, and the relationship between structure and function. That foundation continues to shape her interest in joint health — and the physician-directed, supportive joint protocols she designs today with the whole patient in mind.

These protocols complement, rather than replace, care from a rheumatologist, orthopedist or primary physician.

2004 — 2008

Anesthesiology residency

Nassau University Medical Center · East Meadow, New York

Anesthesiology taught Dr. Aronov that IV medicine leaves no room for casual decisions. During four years of intensive training in physiology, pharmacology, airway management and critical care, she learned to recognize subtle changes, anticipate complications and act decisively when seconds matter.

That same discipline now guides every patient screening, medication selection, dose and infusion she personally oversees.

Clinical training affiliates Winthrop University Hospital South Nassau Communities Hospital St. Francis Hospital

2008 — Present

Nearly two decades in the operating room

General · OB/GYN · Vascular · GI · Pediatric · Orthopedic · Urology

What does nearly two decades in the operating room teach you about an IV? That the bag is the simplest part.

In clinical anesthesiology, every medication, fluid and electrolyte can affect breathing, blood pressure, heart rhythm and cardiovascular stability — sometimes within seconds. Dr. Aronov’s work has required her to anticipate those changes, recognize subtle warning signs, and respond before they become emergencies.

That experience changes how you approach an IV. You do not begin with what can be added to the bag. You begin with the person receiving it — their medical history, medications, symptoms, hydration status, and whether an infusion is appropriate at all.

Alpha Vital Elite brings that operating-room standard into an outpatient setting — to you.

Chapter II The Alpha Vital approach

Four principles, applied to every infusion.

Physician-directed. Personally delivered.

At Alpha Vital, “physician-directed” is more than a title — it is the standard. Dr. Aronov personally reviews your medical screening, determines each dose, prepares your infusion, and manages its administration from start to finish, remaining present and attentive throughout your visit.

Individually screened

Before any infusion is considered, your medical history, medications, allergies, current concerns and relevant laboratory findings are carefully reviewed. Because the most important decision is not simply which infusion to choose — but whether an infusion is medically appropriate for you at all.

Physician-supervised throughout

Your vital signs are monitored as clinically indicated in a private, calm and unhurried setting. Your physician remains on-site throughout your infusion, providing attentive medical oversight from the moment you settle into your recliner until you are ready to leave. This is not a walk-in IV bar — it is physician-directed care.

Supportive by design — never a substitute

Our role is to complement — not compete with — the medical care you already receive. When clinically appropriate, we coordinate with your treating physicians so any wellness infusion fits responsibly within your broader care plan. IV wellness support never replaces evaluation, diagnosis or ongoing care from your primary physician or specialist. If an infusion is not appropriate for you, we will say so — clearly.

Why Alpha Vital exists

The attention used in a hospital should not stop at the hospital door.

In New York City alone, hundreds of locations market IV drips as wellness products. Many are staffed primarily by aestheticians or nurses and supervised remotely by a medical director the patient may never meet. Many offer standardised menu drips with no individual review of history, allergies, medications or clinical considerations. Alpha Vital Elite is built on a different premise.
