Where hydrogen may help

Support for everyday wellness.

Molecular hydrogen is the smallest and lightest molecule. Researchers are studying its selective antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity in five main areas.

  1. 01

    Breathing

    Respiratory comfort and breathing support

    Because hydrogen diffuses rapidly across lung membranes and into cells, it is being studied for its ability to calm airway inflammation and protect lung tissue from oxidative stress.

  2. 02

    Recovery

    Respiratory wellness after viral illness

    In a randomized, placebo-controlled study of adults recovering from COVID-19, two weeks of hydrogen inhalation was associated with better walking distance and lung-function measures.

  3. 03

    Resilience

    Comfort during the cancer treatment journey

    Small studies alongside oncology care have reported less fatigue, nausea and other treatment-related symptoms. The evidence is preliminary, and sessions during active treatment require approval from your oncologist.

  4. 04

    Brain

    Cognitive and neurological wellness support

    Early human and laboratory research is exploring hydrogen’s antioxidant effects in the brain and nervous system. Findings so far are preliminary.

  5. 05

    Heart

    Cardiovascular recovery and heart health support

    Hospital-based studies have examined hydrogen inhalation after heart attack and cardiac arrest, alongside standard emergency care.

A quiet, cumulative practice — like sleep or hydration, the difference tends to show up over time.

Most of the studies listed below took place in hospitals or laboratories, with different devices, concentrations, session lengths and patient groups than a wellness session. They show what researchers are investigating; they do not predict how sessions will affect you. Individual results vary and are not guaranteed.

The basics

Is hydrogen inhalation safe?

Hydrogen inhalation has been well tolerated in the studies to date. Molecular hydrogen is a naturally occurring gas — your gut already produces it during digestion, so the body is used to it — and it leaves no toxic byproducts: when it neutralizes free radicals, it simply becomes water. It is also selective, targeting the most harmful free radicals while leaving beneficial ones alone, so it does not interfere with normal cell function. Sessions are gentle and non-invasive, and the device we use has been approved for home use.

Within a wellness setting, standard precautions still apply. Hydrogen mixed with air can ignite at concentrations of about 4% and above, so wellness equipment must be certified to deliver safe, low-concentration mixtures. At our clinic, we strictly follow the manufacturer’s recommendation and never go above 4%.

Is it odorless and tasteless?

Yes. Molecular hydrogen has no smell and no taste, so you won’t notice anything different during a session. It’s the same gas your body produces naturally, in small amounts, during digestion.

How is it administered?

Through gentle inhalation using a simple face mask. You just breathe normally while seated.

Do I need a referral or a screening?

No. Unlike our IV infusions, hydrogen inhalation does not require a referral, a prescription or our online safety screening. It is offered as a general wellness service, so you can request a session directly through our contact form — much like booking a sauna or a massage.

When you arrive, you’ll sign a standard health waiver and intake form confirming that you have no acute medical issues that would make the session inappropriate or uncomfortable.

Can I combine hydrogen with my current wellness routine?

Absolutely. Hydrogen works alongside most other practices — exercise, nutrition, meditation, physical therapy, whatever you’re already doing. It doesn’t replace anything; it simply adds another layer of antioxidant support. Just let us know what else you’re using so everything stays in sync.

Are the results immediate?

Not usually. Hydrogen is more of a cumulative practice. Some people notice subtle shifts in energy or breathing within a few sessions, but the deeper benefits tend to build over weeks of consistent use. Think of it like sleep or hydration: one good night helps, but the real difference shows up over time.

Your session

What happens during my first visit?

You’ll start with a short consultation about your goals and health background. Then you’ll settle into a comfortable chair, put on your face mask and breathe for your chosen session length. We’ll check in with you throughout, and at the end we’ll talk about what to expect going forward and schedule your next visit if you choose to continue.

How long is a session?

Anywhere from 30 to 90 minutes, depending on what you’re looking for. You can choose your preferred length on the day.

What should I expect to feel?

Most people feel nothing at all: no tingling, no pressure, just quiet breathing. Some describe a sense of calm or lightness afterward, but there’s no dramatic sensation in the moment. It’s subtle, which is exactly the point.

Is there any downtime afterward?

No. You can drive yourself home, go straight back to work or carry on with your day. There’s no recovery period, because nothing about the process sedates or exhausts you.

How often should I come in?

It depends on your goals. Many people start with two to three sessions a week for the first few weeks, then adjust based on how they feel. Your first visit is a good time to map out a rhythm that fits your schedule and your body’s response.

Can I eat or drink before a session?

You generally do not need to fast; a light meal beforehand is fine. Avoid a large meal immediately before reclining if it makes you uncomfortable, and drink water as you normally would. Follow any dietary or fluid restrictions provided by your treating physician.

What should I wear?

Comfortable clothing that feels loose around your chest and neck. You stay in your own clothes while seated and breathing through your fitted face mask.

Safety & who it’s for

Who should avoid hydrogen inhalation?

While hydrogen is generally well tolerated, there are situations where it’s best to hold off or get medical clearance first. It is not for:

  • People with severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) or other conditions where oxygen levels are tightly controlled by a physician
  • Anyone with a pneumothorax (collapsed lung) or a recent chest injury
  • Anyone with a history of seizures that aren’t fully managed
  • People who are pregnant or breastfeeding, simply because safety data in those groups is limited
  • Anyone currently on supplemental oxygen therapy at home
  • People with severe asthma that is unstable or has recently flared
  • Anyone who has had recent ear or sinus surgery, since changes in pressure and gas can affect healing
  • Anyone experiencing chest pain, shortness of breath or signs of a heart event — that’s an emergency-room situation, not a wellness session

The common thread: if your breathing or oxygen balance is already being managed medically, hydrogen inhalation needs a doctor’s sign-off before you begin.

When should I postpone a session or seek urgent care?

Postpone your appointment if you have a fever, a potentially contagious illness, worsening asthma or COPD symptoms, or another new or unstable medical problem.

New or severe shortness of breath, chest pain, fainting, confusion or blue lips require urgent medical assessment. Call 911 for severe symptoms.

Does hydrogen interact with medications?

There’s no known direct interaction, because hydrogen works at the cellular level rather than through the same pathways most drugs use. Still, if you’re taking blood thinners, immunosuppressants or anything for a heart condition, mention it at your first visit so we can be thorough.

Can children use hydrogen inhalation?

It’s generally considered gentle enough for older children and teens, but we’d want a parent or guardian present and, ideally, a pediatrician’s input first. We don’t recommend it for infants or toddlers without specialist guidance.

Can children with asthma receive it?

That’s an important distinction. For a child whose asthma is well controlled with medication and who isn’t having frequent flare-ups, hydrogen inhalation may be something to explore with their pediatrician on board.

But if the asthma is active, recently worsened or requires emergency inhaler use, that’s exactly the kind of situation where we’d say not yet. The priority is always the child’s breathing stability first.

Respiratory wellness & cancer care

Why is inhaled hydrogen considered for respiratory wellness?

Researchers are interested in inhaled molecular hydrogen for respiratory wellness because of its selective antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Because it is the smallest and lightest molecule, inhaled hydrogen diffuses rapidly across lung membranes and into cells and their organelles.

What areas of respiratory wellness are being studied?
  • Airway inflammation. Laboratory studies in models of asthma and COPD have found less airway inflammation after hydrogen inhalation.
  • Targeted antioxidant protection. Rather than neutralizing all free radicals — some of which the immune system needs — hydrogen selectively targets the highly reactive hydroxyl radical and peroxynitrite, helping protect lung cells from oxidative stress.
  • Easier airflow. Hydrogen has exceptionally low density. In hospital research, high-concentration hydrogen–oxygen mixtures lowered resistance to airflow and the effort needed to breathe. Wellness devices, including ours, use far lower concentrations.
  • Tissue scarring. Laboratory research is exploring possible anti-fibrotic effects in lung tissue.
Can hydrogen inhalation be considered during cancer treatment?

Cancer treatment can be physically and emotionally demanding, and it is understandable to look for additional ways to feel supported along the way.

Researchers are studying inhaled molecular hydrogen alongside oncology care. Some studies have reported improvements in fatigue, nausea or other treatment-related symptoms, but the evidence remains preliminary and does not establish benefit for every cancer type or treatment.

What about a session before chemotherapy?

Before considering a session during active cancer treatment, we require physician review and approval from your treating oncologist. Any proposed use and timing must be reviewed with your oncology team. Continue all prescribed cancer treatments and medicines for side effects as directed.

If you or someone you love is undergoing treatment, contact us to discuss your questions and whether further medical review is appropriate.
