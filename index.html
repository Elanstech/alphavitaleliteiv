Living with a chronic condition

A diagnosis is not
a closed door.

Many of the people who come here already have a condition, a consultant and a prescription. What they are usually looking for is nutritional and antioxidant support that sits beside that care rather than competing with it. Every entry below names the compound, states what it does, and is explicit about where the claim stops.

i

An anesthesiologist reads physiology under stress

Nearly two decades in the operating room means hydration status, blood pressure, inflammation, blood loss, medication response and electrolyte shifts are the daily language — not a new vocabulary learned for a wellness menu.

ii

The vein is the native instrument

Anesthesiology is intravenous medicine. Dose calculation by weight and physiology, controlled infusion rate, and continuous monitoring are the ordinary mechanics of the specialty, applied here.

iii

Every ingredient has to justify its place

An ingredient earns its way into the bag by solving a specific problem — a likely deficiency, poor absorption, or unusual losses. A vague request to feel better is not a reason to add anything.

Conditions we are asked about

What is actually going on

Crohn’s most often inflames the terminal ileum — the exact stretch of bowel where vitamin B12 and bile salts are absorbed. Inflammation, surgical resection and faster transit all mean less of what you eat actually reaches the bloodstream. Low iron, B12, folate, vitamin D and zinc are documented in inflammatory bowel disease, and studies find low zinc in roughly 15 to 40 out of every 100 people with IBD.

The active components, and why

  • Zinc

    An essential mineral that supports hundreds of enzymes, immune-cell communication and normal tissue repair. It is absorbed in the small bowel — the stretch Crohn’s most often involves.

  • B-Complex & B12

    B12 is taken up in the terminal ileum. When that segment is inflamed or resected, oral intake is an unreliable route.

  • Amino Acids

    Building blocks for protein synthesis, which reduced intake and prolonged flares can leave short.

Why the line

The rationale here is about the route, not the disease. Nutrients delivered by line do not have to survive an inflamed gut. That is a delivery argument, and it is the only argument being made.

What this is not

This does not treat Crohn’s disease. Biologics, immunomodulators and steroids do. Stopping any of them in favour of an infusion would be a serious mistake, and Dr. Aronov will tell you so.

What is actually going on

UC involves the colon and rectum. Ongoing blood loss makes iron deficiency the most common complication outside the gut itself, and frequent stools carry off fluid and electrolytes faster than drinking can replace them — particularly during a flare. Reduced intake during those periods compounds it.

The active components, and why

  • Hydration Fluid & Electrolytes

    Replacing measurable losses. This is the least speculative use of a line anywhere on this page.

  • B-Complex & Amino Acids

    Nutritional support during periods when intake and absorption are both reduced.

  • Glutathione & Alpha-Lipoic Acid

    Antioxidant-network compounds that support normal cellular redox balance.

Why the line

Replacing what is being lost is supportive care, and it is honest about being exactly that. If iron deficiency is the real issue, that is a diagnosis your gastroenterologist makes and treats — intravenous iron is their prescription, not a wellness infusion.

What this is not

This does not induce remission and it does not maintain it. Nothing offered here changes the course of the disease.

What is actually going on

In newly diagnosed celiac disease the injured intestinal lining may not absorb zinc well — some studies put around half of patients at risk. The same problem appears after bariatric or bowel surgery, where there is simply less absorbing surface, and in chronic pancreatitis, short bowel and cystic fibrosis.

The active components, and why

  • Trace Minerals

    Zinc, copper, manganese and selenium — the minerals a shortened or injured bowel stops taking up reliably.

  • B-Complex & Mineral Blend

    B1, B2, B3, B5, B6 and B12 with magnesium chloride and calcium gluconate.

  • Vitamin C

    An antioxidant that is also required for normal collagen production.

Why the line

When the gut is the bottleneck, the line removes the bottleneck. Dr. Aronov still asks the harder question first — whether a working gut could do the job, because if it can, food and oral supplementation are the simpler road.

What this is not

A gluten-free diet remains the treatment for celiac disease. An infusion does not repair villi, and it does not make gluten safe.

What is actually going on

RA is systemic autoimmune disease, not worn joints. Methotrexate — still the anchor drug — works by interfering with folate, which is why folic acid is prescribed alongside it. Biologics deliberately switch off specific parts of the immune response. Nutritional needs can shift with diet, inflammation and medication.

The active components, and why

  • Collagen Amino Acids

    Glycine, proline and lysine are involved in normal collagen formation, including the type II collagen of joint cartilage.

  • Vitamin C

    Required for normal collagen production, and an antioxidant — a dual role in connective-tissue metabolism.

  • Copper

    A cofactor for the enzymes involved in collagen cross-linking, the process that gives collagen its structure.

  • Selenium

    Incorporated into glutathione peroxidases, the enzymes that use glutathione to neutralise oxidative compounds.

Why the line

Amino acids supply the building materials; vitamin C, copper, zinc and magnesium help the body process them; NAC, selenium, ALA and glutathione support the antioxidant system around those normal processes. A coordinated support team, not a single hero ingredient.

What this is not

This does not stop autoimmune joint damage and it is not a DMARD. Folate management for anyone taking methotrexate must stay coordinated with your rheumatologist. Controlling the underlying disease remains essential.

What is actually going on

Cartilage is a matrix, and type II collagen is one of its principal structural components. The body maintains that matrix continuously using amino acids, minerals and enzyme cofactors drawn from what you eat and absorb.

The active components, and why

  • Glycine, Proline & Lysine

    The amino acids involved in normal collagen formation — type II in cartilage, types I and III in skin.

  • Magnesium

    Supports hundreds of enzyme reactions including cellular energy production, protein synthesis and the body’s own glutathione production.

  • Zinc & Copper, balanced

    Zinc supports protein production, cell division and tissue maintenance. It is kept in balance with copper, because excess zinc interferes with copper absorption.

Why the line

This is a nutritional-support argument: supply the materials the body uses for normal connective-tissue maintenance, and support the antioxidant system operating around those processes.

What this is not

Supplying nutrients involved in collagen production is not the same as proving the body will produce new cartilage. This protocol does not replace or regenerate destroyed cartilage, and it does not treat or cure osteoarthritis.

What is actually going on

Psoriasis is not a skin problem that happens to be visible — it is systemic immune-mediated inflammation that shows up on the skin. Around a third of patients develop psoriatic arthritis, and metabolic and cardiovascular risk run higher than in the general population.

The active components, and why

  • Type I & III Collagen Amino Acids

    The skin’s structural layer depends on collagen in the same way cartilage does, which is why one infusion serves both.

  • N-Acetylcysteine

    Supplies cysteine, one of the amino acids the body needs to make glutathione. NAC supports the body’s own production pathway.

  • Glutathione & Selenium

    A major intracellular antioxidant working alongside the selenium-dependent enzymes of the same defence system.

Why the line

Supportive care alongside your prescribed therapy, with your dermatologist informed. It supports the skin’s nutritional and antioxidant needs — that is the whole claim.

What this is not

This is not a treatment for psoriasis and does not guarantee skin healing. It does not clear plaques — topicals, phototherapy, systemics and biologics do, and they belong to your dermatologist.

What is actually going on

Metabolic fatty liver disease is now the most common chronic liver condition in the world. Everything absorbed from your gut is routed through the liver first, which is exactly why liver health sits at the centre of how the body handles anything you take in.

The active components, and why

  • Glycine & Taurine

    Amino acids the liver uses to prepare bile — the digestive fluid that helps the body process fats.

  • N-Acetylcysteine & Glycine

    Together they supply the building blocks the body uses to make glutathione. NAC is a real drug, used in hospitals for decades.

  • Alpha-Lipoic Acid

    Participates in mitochondrial energy metabolism and supports the interconnected antioxidant network with vitamin C and glutathione.

  • Magnesium & B-Complex

    Involved in normal cellular energy and enzyme function — the metabolic foundation that lets the rest be processed and used.

Why the line

Liver health is not a one-day detox. Your liver works around the clock, and one infusion cannot undo months or years of metabolic stress. The six-week structure exists so tolerance can be monitored and the plan adjusted.

The IV Advantage

Same destination.
Very different roads.

A vitamin only works once it reaches your bloodstream. Swallow it and it has to survive the trip. Put it in a vein and it is simply already there. That is the whole difference — and it is why some things are worth giving by line.

By mouth

Four stops before it counts

4stops
  1. 1

    Your stomach

    Acid and enzymes start breaking the tablet apart. Some of what you swallowed does not survive this room.

  2. 2

    Your gut wall

    Whatever is left has to cross into the blood. How much crosses depends on the nutrient, your gut, and what else you ate.

  3. 3

    Your liver

    Everything absorbed from the gut is routed through the liver first, and the liver takes its cut before passing the rest on.

  4. 4

    Your bloodstream

    What finally arrives is some fraction of the dose on the label — and it gets there over hours, not minutes.

The catch: you never quite know how much made it. So the label dose is a guess dressed up as a number.

By line

Straight in, no stops

0stops

  1. Into the vein

    The infusion goes directly into circulation. There is no stomach to survive and no gut wall to cross.

  2. Your bloodstream

    The full dose is in circulation within minutes, and Dr. Aronov controls how fast it goes in the entire time.

The point: she knows exactly what went in, because she calculated it, mixed it and watched it go.

This is not an argument against swallowing your vitamins. For most people, most of the time, by mouth is exactly right — it is cheaper, easier, and it works. The line matters when absorption is unreliable, when the dose has to be certain, or when someone is too depleted to wait. Which of those applies to you is a question for the screening, not for a website.
