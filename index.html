Chapter I Our Services

Infusions, injections
& inhalation, individually reviewed.

Every IV infusion is physician-prepared, and each ingredient is given from its own separate bag rather than mixed together in one. That way every component stays on its own, and Dr. Aronov controls how each one is delivered. Before scheduling, Dr. Aronov reviews your medical history, medications, allergies, and individual needs to determine whether IV support is right for you—and may recommend a signature infusion, modify the formula, or create a customized option. IV support is intended to complement—not replace—care from your primary care physician or specialist.

Vitamin D3 and B12 are given as intramuscular (IM) shots—a quick, nearly painless injection into the muscle of your arm or hip. The muscle absorbs and releases the vitamin directly, skipping the digestive system. Glutathione is different: it is given as a slow IV push straight into a vein over about 15 minutes, on its own—not mixed into a bag of IV fluids. Every injection is physician-reviewed and directed.

Hydrogen inhalation is completely needle-free. You relax in a quiet, hands-free session for 30 to 90 minutes while you breathe a gentle hydrogen blend through your own personal mask—no IV, no downtime. A brief health screening is required before your first session, and sessions are available Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

Chapter IV Common questions

Before you
get in touch.

Three of the questions we hear most. Forty answers, including the questions for every infusion, are on the questions page.

Do I need an appointment?

Yes. All visits are by appointment only; walk-ins are not accepted. Alpha Vital Elite is an outpatient medical wellness practice, not a hospital. Before an infusion can be scheduled, a physician must review your health information to determine whether the nutritional and antioxidant IV support we offer falls within our scope and can be provided safely and appropriately in our setting. If your medical needs fall outside our services, you may be advised to seek care from an appropriate physician or medical facility.

Is there a physician review fee?

No. There is no screening or physician review fee. Before an infusion can be scheduled, a physician reviews your safety-screening answers, medical history, medications, allergies, relevant laboratory results or medical records, and wellness goals at no charge. This individualized review helps determine whether the IV support available at Alpha Vital Elite is safe and appropriate for you — and, when appropriate, which option may best meet your needs.

The screening itself is a short questionnaire that takes only a few minutes. If IV support is appropriate, Dr. Violeta Aronov contacts you directly to schedule your appointment. The complete medical form and patient attestation are reviewed and signed at the clinic on the day of your infusion.

Occasionally, the review may show that additional information is needed or that the services available at our practice are not the right fit for you. In that situation, the physician will explain why and, when appropriate, whether another option may be considered.

Who personally provides and monitors my infusion?

At Alpha Vital Elite, physician involvement continues throughout your visit. Your infusion is personally provided and monitored by Dr. Violeta Aronov or another physician on the Alpha Vital Elite medical team.

The physician providing your care reviews your medical history, determines and verifies the appropriate formulation and doses, prepares and starts your infusion, sets the flow rate, and remains with you through completion. Your infusion is not handed off to a technician; a physician remains directly responsible for your care from start to finish.

The Route

Swallowed, it has to survive the trip. In a vein, it is already there.

The Pill Has To Make The Trip

The number on the supplement label tells you how much is inside the pill. It does not tell you exactly how much your body will absorb. Before a swallowed nutrient can reach your blood, it must travel through your digestive system and cross the gut wall. How much gets through can vary.

  1. 1
    Your Stomach

    Stomach acid and digestive juices break the pill apart and release what is inside. Some ingredients make it through the stomach easily. Others may be changed or broken down before your body can absorb them.

  2. 2
    Your Gut Wall

    Think of the gut wall as a row of tiny doors. The nutrient must cross this wall to enter your blood. How much gets through can depend on the nutrient, its form and dose, your digestive health, and what else you eat or take.

  3. 3
    Your Liver

    Many swallowed substances travel to the liver before reaching the rest of the body. The liver may change, store, or remove part of some ingredients.

  4. 4
    Your Bloodstream

    What reaches your bloodstream is the amount your body absorbed. In some cases, that may be only 10 to 20 percent of the dose promised on the bottle’s label. It usually arrives gradually over hours.

The Catch — The label tells you what you swallowed. It cannot tell you exactly what your body absorbed.

Through The IV — 0 Stops

  1. Into The Vein

    The infusion enters the vein and joins your bloodstream directly. No stomach to survive. No gut wall to cross.

  2. Your Bloodstream

    Within minutes, the ingredients are moving through your bloodstream. Your blood carries them throughout the body, where tissues can take up what they can use.

The Point — With a pill, you know how much was swallowed. Through an IV, you know how much was delivered directly into circulation.

Why The Sequence Matters

1

Chemistry

What can share a bag?

Some IV ingredients work well together. Others can change or lose strength when they are mixed. Knowing their names is not enough — we check how the actual products behave together before placing them in the same bag.

2

Light Sensitivity

Why we protect certain ingredients

Riboflavin (vitamin B2), ascorbic acid (vitamin C), alpha-lipoic acid, vitamin B12, and glutathione can be sensitive to light. Light can weaken them during preparation and infusion. We use a special cover during infusion to protect them — just as dark bottles protect some medicines.

3

Infusion Control

Why separate IV bags matter

Different IV ingredients may need to be infused at different speeds. Putting them in separate bags lets us control how fast each stage is given. If a patient has an adverse reaction to a particular IV ingredient, the physician can slow or stop that stage and more easily identify which ingredient may be causing it. Your safety is our number 1 priority.

4

Traceable Infusion Stages

Because your safety matters

When ingredients are given in separate bags, each stage is traceable. If any reaction begins — whether it is an allergy or something else — we know what was being infused. We can stop that stage and better identify the possible cause.

An ingredient list tells you what is being given. Our process also considers what belongs together, what needs protection from light, the order in which each stage should be given, how fast it should run, and how a problem could be traced.

Chemistry. Light. Control. Traceability. Every step has a reason.
