The IV Advantage

Same destination.
Very different roads.

A vitamin only works once it reaches your bloodstream. Swallow it and it has to survive the trip. Put it in a vein and it is simply already there. That is the whole difference — and it is why some things are worth giving by line.

By mouth

Four stops before it counts

4stops
  1. 1

    Your stomach

    Acid and enzymes start breaking the tablet apart. Some of what you swallowed does not survive this room.

  2. 2

    Your gut wall

    Whatever is left has to cross into the blood. How much crosses depends on the nutrient, your gut, and what else you ate.

  3. 3

    Your liver

    Everything absorbed from the gut is routed through the liver first, and the liver takes its cut before passing the rest on.

  4. 4

    Your bloodstream

    What finally arrives is some fraction of the dose on the label — and it gets there over hours, not minutes.

The catch: you never quite know how much made it. So the label dose is a guess dressed up as a number.

By line

Straight in, no stops

0stops

  1. Into the vein

    The infusion goes directly into circulation. There is no stomach to survive and no gut wall to cross.

  2. Your bloodstream

    The full dose is in circulation within minutes, and Dr. Aronov controls how fast it goes in the entire time.

The point: she knows exactly what went in, because she calculated it, mixed it and watched it go.

This is not an argument against swallowing your vitamins. For most people, most of the time, by mouth is exactly right — it is cheaper, easier, and it works. The line matters when absorption is unreliable, when the dose has to be certain, or when someone is too depleted to wait. Which of those applies to you is a question for the screening, not for a website.
