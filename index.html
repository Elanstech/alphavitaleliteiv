The IV Advantage

Same destination.
Very different roads.

A vitamin only works once it reaches your bloodstream. Swallow it and it has to survive the trip. Put it in a vein and it is simply already there. That is the whole difference — and it is why some things are worth giving by line.

By mouth

Four stops before it counts

4stops
  1. 1

    Your stomach

    Acid and enzymes start breaking the tablet apart. Some of what you swallowed does not survive this room.

  2. 2

    Your gut wall

    Whatever is left has to cross into the blood. How much crosses depends on the nutrient, your gut, and what else you ate.

  3. 3

    Your liver

    Everything absorbed from the gut is routed through the liver first, and the liver takes its cut before passing the rest on.

  4. 4

    Your bloodstream

    What finally arrives is some fraction of the dose on the label — and it gets there over hours, not minutes.

The catch: you never quite know how much made it. So the label dose is a guess dressed up as a number.

By line

Straight in, no stops

0stops

  1. Into the vein

    The infusion goes directly into circulation. There is no stomach to survive and no gut wall to cross.

  2. Your bloodstream

    The full dose is in circulation within minutes, and Dr. Aronov controls how fast it goes in the entire time.

The point: she knows exactly what went in, because she calculated it, mixed it and watched it go.

This is not an argument against swallowing your vitamins. For most people, most of the time, by mouth is exactly right — it is cheaper, easier, and it works. The line matters when absorption is unreliable, when the dose has to be certain, or when someone is too depleted to wait. Which of those applies to you is a question for the screening, not for a website.

Conditions we are asked about

I

Crohn’s disease

Requires sign-off from your gastroenterologist

What is actually going on

Crohn’s most often inflames the terminal ileum — the exact stretch of bowel where vitamin B12 and bile salts are absorbed. Inflammation, surgical resection and faster transit all mean less of what you eat actually gets in. Deficiencies in iron, B12, folate, vitamin D and zinc are common and well documented in IBD, and a flare adds reduced intake and real fluid loss on top.

Where a line may support you

Fluids and nutrients delivered by line do not have to survive an inflamed gut. That is the honest rationale — it is about the route, not about the disease. N-acetylcysteine, as a glutathione precursor, is an area of genuine research interest in inflammatory bowel disease, but the evidence is early.

What it will never be

It does not treat Crohn’s. Biologics, immunomodulators and steroids do. Stopping any of them in favour of an infusion would be a serious mistake, and Dr. Aronov will tell you so.

II

Ulcerative colitis

Requires sign-off from your gastroenterologist

What is actually going on

UC involves the colon and rectum. Ongoing blood loss makes iron deficiency the most common complication outside the gut itself, and frequent stools carry off fluid and electrolytes faster than drinking can replace them — particularly during a flare.

Where a line may support you

Hydration and electrolyte replacement is the clearest, least speculative use of a line here. If iron deficiency is the issue, that is a diagnosis your gastroenterologist makes and treats — intravenous iron is their prescription, not a wellness infusion.

What it will never be

It does not induce remission and it does not maintain it. Nothing offered here changes the course of the disease.

III

Rheumatoid arthritis

Requires sign-off from your rheumatologist

What is actually going on

RA is systemic autoimmune disease, not worn joints. Methotrexate — still the anchor drug — works by interfering with folate, which is why folic acid is prescribed alongside it. Biologics deliberately switch off specific parts of the immune response.

Where a line may support you

This is precisely why the sign-off matters more than the formula. Timing has to sit sensibly around your infusion or injection schedule, and nobody should assume that adding anything is neutral in a person who is deliberately immunosuppressed.

What it will never be

It does not replace DMARDs or biologics, and it does not slow joint damage. Your rheumatologist owns the disease; this sits beside their plan or not at all.

IV

Psoriasis

Requires sign-off from your dermatologist

What is actually going on

Psoriasis is not a skin problem that happens to be visible — it is systemic immune-mediated inflammation that shows up on the skin. Around a third of patients develop psoriatic arthritis, and metabolic and cardiovascular risk run higher than in the general population.

Where a line may support you

Supportive care alongside your prescribed therapy, with your dermatologist informed. Antioxidant and glutathione-pathway support is of research interest in inflammatory skin disease; it is not established treatment and will not be presented as such.

What it will never be

It does not clear plaques. Topicals, phototherapy, systemics and biologics are the treatments, and they belong to your dermatologist.

V

Fatty liver disease

Requires sign-off from your hepatologist or gastroenterologist

What is actually going on

Metabolic fatty liver disease is now the most common chronic liver condition in the world. Everything absorbed from your gut is routed through the liver first, which is exactly why liver health sits at the centre of how the body handles anything you take.

Where a line may support you

Glycine, N-acetylcysteine, alpha-lipoic acid and glutathione support antioxidant and metabolic pathways relevant to liver health. That is a statement about pathways, not about outcomes, and Dr. Aronov will describe it to you as exactly that.

What it will never be

Be very clear about this one. The intervention with real evidence behind fatty liver is metabolic — sustained weight reduction, glycaemic control and alcohol. An infusion is not a shortcut around that work, and anyone selling it as one is selling you something. She would rather you spend the money on the thing that works.
