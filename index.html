Living with a chronic condition

A diagnosis is not
a closed door.

Nutritional and antioxidant support that sits beside the care you already have. Every entry names the compound, states what it does, and is explicit about where the claim stops — read by an anesthesiologist who has spent nearly two decades reading physiology under stress.

01 / 07 conditions

I

Malabsorption

Crohn’s Disease

Crohn’s most often inflames the terminal ileum — the exact stretch of bowel where vitamin B12 and bile salts are absorbed. Low iron, B12, folate, vitamin D and zinc are documented in inflammatory bowel disease, and studies find low zinc in 15 to 40 of every 100 patients.

Delivered in Immun-O-Boost IV Support 2 h 30·$650

The active components

  • ZincSupports hundreds of enzymes, immune-cell communication and normal tissue repair.
  • B-Complex & B12Taken up in the ileum — the segment most often involved.
  • Amino AcidsBuilding blocks that reduced intake and long flares leave short.

Not: Does not treat Crohn’s. Biologics, immunomodulators and steroids do.

II

Fluid & nutrient loss

Ulcerative Colitis

UC involves the colon and rectum. Ongoing blood loss makes iron deficiency the most common complication outside the gut itself, and frequent stools carry off fluid and electrolytes faster than drinking can replace them.

Delivered in Revive IV Support 2 h 15·$625

The active components

  • Hydration & ElectrolytesReplacing measurable losses — the least speculative use of a line.
  • B-Complex & Amino AcidsSupport while intake and absorption are both reduced.
  • Glutathione & ALAAntioxidant-network compounds supporting normal redox balance.

Not: Does not induce remission and does not maintain it. Nothing offered here changes the course of the disease.

III

Reduced absorbing surface

Celiac & Malabsorption

In newly diagnosed celiac disease the injured intestinal lining may not absorb zinc well — some studies put around half of patients at risk. The same problem follows bariatric or bowel surgery, where there is simply less absorbing surface.

Delivered in Immun-O-Boost IV Support 2 h 30·$650

The active components

  • Trace MineralsZinc, copper, manganese and selenium — what a shortened bowel stops taking up.
  • B-Complex & MineralsB1 through B12 with magnesium chloride and calcium gluconate.
  • Vitamin CAn antioxidant that is also required for normal collagen production.

Not: A gluten-free diet remains the treatment. An infusion does not repair villi.

IV

Systemic autoimmune inflammation

Rheumatoid Arthritis

RA is autoimmune disease, not worn joints. Methotrexate works by interfering with folate, which is why folic acid is prescribed alongside it. Nutritional needs shift with diet, inflammation and medication.

Delivered in Joint & Skin Wellness IV 2 h 30·$750

The active components

  • Collagen Amino AcidsGlycine, proline and lysine — involved in normal type II collagen formation.
  • Vitamin CRequired for normal collagen production, and an antioxidant.
  • CopperCofactor for the enzymes behind collagen cross-linking.
  • SeleniumBuilt into glutathione peroxidases, the enzymes that use glutathione.

Not: Not a DMARD. Does not stop autoimmune joint damage or slow its progression.

V

Cartilage matrix maintenance

Osteoarthritis

Cartilage is a matrix, and type II collagen is one of its principal structural components. The body maintains that matrix continuously, using amino acids, minerals and enzyme cofactors drawn from what you absorb.

Delivered in Joint & Skin Wellness IV 2 h 30·$750

The active components

  • Glycine, Proline & LysineThe amino acids involved in normal collagen formation.
  • MagnesiumSupports energy production, protein synthesis and glutathione production.
  • Zinc & Copper, balancedExcess zinc interferes with copper absorption, so the two are kept in ratio.

Not: Supplying nutrients involved in collagen production is not the same as producing new cartilage. This does not replace or regenerate destroyed cartilage.

VI

Immune-mediated skin inflammation

Psoriasis

Psoriasis is systemic immune-mediated inflammation that shows up on the skin. Around a third of patients develop psoriatic arthritis, and metabolic and cardiovascular risk run higher than in the general population.

Delivered in Joint & Skin Wellness IV 2 h 30·$750

The active components

  • Type I & III Collagen AminosThe skin’s structural layer depends on collagen as cartilage does.
  • N-AcetylcysteineSupplies cysteine — one amino acid the body needs to make glutathione.
  • Glutathione & SeleniumA major intracellular antioxidant and the enzymes that work with it.

Not: Not a treatment for psoriasis, and no guaranteed skin healing.

VII

Metabolic & oxidative load

Fatty Liver Disease

Now the most common chronic liver condition in the world. Everything absorbed from your gut is routed through the liver first, which is why liver health sits at the centre of how the body handles anything you take in.

Delivered in Fatty Liver Support IV 3 h·$850

The active components

  • Glycine & TaurineAmino acids the liver uses to prepare bile, which helps process fats.
  • NAC & GlycineTogether they supply the building blocks the body uses to make glutathione.
  • Alpha-Lipoic AcidWorks in mitochondrial energy metabolism and the antioxidant network.
  • Magnesium & B-ComplexThe metabolic foundation that lets the rest be processed and used.

Not: Not a detox and not a shortcut. The evidence behind fatty liver is metabolic — weight, glycaemic control and alcohol.

The IV advantage

Same destination.
Very different roads.

A vitamin only works once it reaches your bloodstream. Swallow it and it has to survive the trip. Put it in a vein and it is simply already there. Pick a road and watch it run.

  1. 1

    Your stomach

    Acid and enzymes start breaking the tablet apart. Some of what you swallowed does not survive this room.

  2. 2

    Your gut wall

    Whatever is left has to cross into the blood. How much crosses depends on the nutrient, your gut, and what else you ate.

  3. 3

    Your liver

    Everything absorbed from the gut is routed through the liver first. It takes its share before passing the rest on.

  4. 4

    Your bloodstream

    What finally arrives is some fraction of the dose on the label — and it arrives over hours.

The catch — you never quite know how much made it. The label dose is an estimate wearing a number.

  1. Into the vein

    The infusion goes directly into circulation. No stomach to survive, no gut wall to cross.

  2. Your bloodstream

    The dose is in circulation within minutes, and Dr. Aronov controls the rate the entire time.

The point — she knows exactly what went in, because she calculated it, mixed it and watched it go.

This is not an argument against swallowing your vitamins. For most people, most of the time, by mouth is exactly right — cheaper, easier, and it works. The line matters when absorption is unreliable, when the dose has to be certain, or when someone is too depleted to wait. Which of those applies to you is a question for the screening, not for a website.

Chapter III Before you come in

No walk-ins.
That is the point.

Four steps sit between your first message and the chair — every one of them performed by the physician who will be in the room with you.

  1. 01

    You send your history

    Medical history, current medications, allergies. A few minutes. Nothing is booked and nothing is charged at this stage.

  2. 02

    She reads it herself

    Dr. Aronov reviews what you sent and decides whether IV therapy is appropriate — and which formula fits.

  3. 03

    Eligibility, decided case by case

    There is no blanket rule. She weighs your history, your medications and your goals, and decides what is appropriate for you specifically — or that nothing here is.

  4. 04

    Your appointment

    She calculates the doses, prepares the medications, sets the rate, and stays in the room until it is finished.

Not everyone is eligible. If IV therapy is not appropriate for you, she will say so, and tell you why.
